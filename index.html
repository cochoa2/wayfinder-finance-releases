What it is

A local-only personal finance and retirement planner

1

What it answers

  • Where does my money actually go each month, and how much am I really saving?
  • Will my savings last through retirement, and what pays for each year once I stop working?
  • How much should I put into each kid's 529?
  • What happens if I take a sabbatical, move to another state, or buy a rental?
2

Who it's for

  • The person in a household who does the planning who sits down every few weeks for a deliberate session at a desktop or laptop
  • Each session starts with what changed, and what needs you import new statements, check that spending is categorized, see whether the plan still holds
  • Explained at the level you choose plain English, comfortable or expert: the same figures at three depths, never three different answers
3

What it needs from you

  • Your household's facts a setup wizard asks for ages, filing status, state, income and contributions
  • Your accounts and balances added by hand, or from a brokerage positions export
  • Statement CSVs from your bank's website three or more months gives meaningful spending numbers; twelve is better

Every page draws on one engine, so a number means the same thing wherever it appears.

What each page answers

Each page opens with its headline figures, then up to three numbered readings

Overview

Dashboard

Where do things stand right now?

Net worth, monthly surplus, savings rate and retirement success at a glance. How your wealth splits across tax buckets, how this month's spending is pacing, and a Path to FI: your FI number, how far along you are, and Coast FI.

A list of things that need attention: uncategorized transactions, stale balances, price increases, and the yearly check-in when it's due.

Money

Cash Flow

Where does my money go?

Monthly spending by type (fixed, everyday essentials, discretionary), and a Sankey diagram of where income went: from gross pay through taxes and savings taken from pay, to spending by kind and what was left over. Click any month to see and fix the transactions behind it.

  • Transactions: sort uncategorized merchants, search and edit anything. A fix can apply to the whole merchant and to future imports.
  • Recurring: bills and subscriptions found automatically, with cadence, next expected date and price changes.
  • Budgets: a pace marker per category. Bills like rent are judged on the full month.
  • Import and Rules: drop in statement CSVs; repeated files and overlapping rows are skipped. Preview exactly which transactions a rule will change before you save it.
Money

Accounts

What do I own, and where does it sit?

Balances by account, how investments split across taxable, traditional, Roth and HSA accounts, and positions imported from brokerage exports. Each account type gets the controls that fit it: positions for investments, one balance for checking and savings, loan terms for debts.

Loans and credit-card balances count against net worth, with a payoff planner that compares extra payments, avalanche and snowball.

Money

Investments

Is my money invested the way I mean it to be?

  • Your mix against a target you choose, and the trades that would rebalance it, made inside 401(k)s, IRAs and HSAs first so selling owes no tax.
  • Asset location: the yearly tax your taxable account's income costs at your rates, and how much less it would be with the same mix placed differently.
  • Fees: each fund's expense ratio, what it costs this year and by retirement, and an option to include fees in the plan's returns.
Money

Goals

Am I saving enough for the things before retirement?

Savings goals with their progress and the monthly amount needed to hit each target date. A goal can take its progress from an account's balance, and needs come before wants in the Advisor's steps.

Planning

Retirement

Will my money last?

Thousands of simulated market paths, shown as a range of outcomes in today's dollars, including taxes, Social Security, required minimum distributions, pre-Medicare healthcare and Medicare IRMAA surcharges. Move any input to explore a what-if; results are compared against your saved plan until you save.

  • Income by age: what pays for each year, and how much goes to taxes.
  • Bridge to 59½: for early retirement, what pays for the years before 401(k) and IRA money is penalty-free.
  • Insights: your path to financial independence and which inputs move your odds the most.
  • Roth conversions and Social Security: strategies side by side, and the result for every claiming age from 62 to 70.
Planning

Advisor

What should I do next, and what if…?

Prioritized recommendations: high-interest debt, emergency fund, idle cash, maxing your 401(k) and HSA, catch-up contributions, backdoor Roth and plan risk. What-if scenarios for a sabbatical, relocating to another state, or buying a rental property, each measured against your saved plan.

This year tracks what you've contributed against your plan and each limit, and holds the yearly check-in.

Planning

College

How much should go into each 529?

Each child's chance of being covered, a recommended monthly contribution, the projected 529 balance by age, and the trade-off between falling short and over-saving.

Assistant

Chat

Can I just ask?

A local AI assistant that answers questions about your finances by running the app's own calculations. It talks to a model on your own computer: Ollama, or an OpenAI-compatible local server such as LM Studio, llama.cpp, Jan, LocalAI or vLLM. Only a port can be set, never another computer.

Optional. Everything else works without a local model.

Settings

Check-in reminders

A nudge when it's been a while.

Off until you turn them on. On Windows the app adds a daily scheduled task that shows a reminder on your desktop when the app hasn't been opened in the time you choose.

Optionally it emails you through your own mail server, or sends a push notification to your own ntfy topic. A reminder says only how long it has been since you last opened the app: no figures, names or accounts.

Also in the app

  • Glossary plain-English definitions of the finance terms used in the app, also shown beside inputs and figures
  • Profile & Setup a setup wizard the first time; income per source and per person; a backup download
  • Any bank's CSV confirm which column is the date, description and amount once, and later files import on their own
  • Display a reading level, a light or dark theme, and four palettes, one chosen to stay distinct for colorblind readers

Privacy and security

Your financial data is about as sensitive as data gets

The app is built to keep your data on your machine.

Local only
The app runs a small web server bound to localhost. Only your own computer can reach it; nothing is exposed on your network or the internet.
No cloud, no accounts, no bank logins
There's no sign-up and no server of ours. The app never connects to your bank: you download statement CSVs from your bank's website yourself and import them.
No telemetry
The app sends nothing about you or how you use it anywhere. Fonts, icons and scripts are bundled with the app, and nothing loads from a CDN.
Local AI only
The optional Chat page talks to a model running on your own computer. Your data is never sent to an online AI service.
Reminders say nothing about your money
Check-in reminders are off until you turn them on. By email or push, they are the one thing the app sends off your computer, and they say only how long it has been since you last opened it.
One file holds everything
All your data lives in a single SQLite file in your home folder. To start over, quit the app and delete that folder. Nothing is stored anywhere else.

The installer includes everything the app needs. The internet is used only to download it, optionally a local model, and to deliver email or push reminders if you turn them on.

What you should do

  • Turn on disk encryption FileVault on macOS, BitLocker or Device Encryption on Windows. The data file itself isn't encrypted.
  • Never share or upload your data file don't email it, put it in a shared folder, or commit it to git
  • Keep backups private Time Machine and File History include the data file automatically
  • Delete statement CSVs from your Downloads folder after importing them if you don't need them

First-time setup

The installers aren't signed with a paid Apple or Microsoft certificate, so your computer shows a one-time warning the first time you open the app.

  1. Install Open the downloaded .dmg and drag Wayfinder Finance onto the Applications folder.
  2. Open it Double-click Wayfinder Finance in Applications. macOS says it “could not verify” the app: click Done, not Move to Trash.
  3. Allow it once
    • Open System Settings → Privacy & Security.
    • Scroll to the message about Wayfinder Finance and click Open Anyway.
    • Enter your Mac password if asked, then click Open Anyway once more.

    On macOS 14 or earlier: right-click the app in Applications, choose Open, then click Open.

  4. You're in Your browser opens to Wayfinder Finance after a few seconds. The very first start can take up to a minute.
  1. Install Double-click Wayfinder-Finance-Windows-Setup.exe.
  2. Allow it once If a blue “Windows protected your PC” message appears, click More info, then Run anyway. You only see this while installing.
  3. Finish the installer No administrator password is needed.
  4. You're in Open Wayfinder Finance from the Start menu or the desktop shortcut. Your browser opens to the app.

Everyday use

  • Start on Profile & Setup then import statement CSVs under Cash Flow → Import
  • Closing it close the browser tab when you're done; the app stops itself about ten minutes later
  • Updating download the newest installer from this page and install it over the old one. Your data is never touched, and it upgrades itself on the next start.

Where your data lives

  • Windows C:\Users\<you>\.wayfinder_finance\data.db
